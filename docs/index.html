EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

A ranking problem, not a prediction claim.

This project supports the decision of which content pages should be reviewed first when a content team cannot optimize every page at once.

A weighted Opportunity Score combines impressions, CTR, average search position, and engagement into a transparent ranking.

The score produces a substantially different top-50 queue from the Week-4 baseline, but the available June-only dataset does not support a claim about future performance.

01
PROBLEM FRAMING

Which pages deserve attention first?

Content teams often have more pages than they can review simultaneously. The practical problem is therefore prioritization.

Which content pages should be prioritized for optimization review?

Unit of analysis

A content page aggregated over the June 2026 observation window.

03

04
MODEL / ANALYSIS

Four signals. One readable score.

The final method is weighted scoring and ranking, rather than a supervised classification model.

OPPORTUNITY SCORE
35% Visibility + 30% CTR Opportunity + 20% Position + 15% Engagement

Each component is normalized before the weighted score is calculated.

Impressions 35%
CTR 30%
Position 20%
Engagement 15%
FEATURES

gsc_impressions, ctr, gsc_avg_position, and engagement_per_session.

TARGET / PROXY

No supervised target is used. The Opportunity Score acts as a prioritization proxy for human content review.

ERROR ANALYSIS

Some high-ranked pages have almost no visibility.

Several highly ranked pages have only a small number of impressions while having very poor average search positions. Poor position contributes positively to the Opportunity Score, but extremely low visibility may make these pages less practical refresh candidates.

These examples demonstrate why the score should be used as a review queue rather than an automatic action list.

06
INTERPRETATION

What the analysis actually found.

The strongest result is a change in prioritization, not a claim of predictive superiority.

01

Different rankings

The Opportunity Score and baseline produce substantially different top-50 queues.

02

Multiple signals matter

Adding CTR, position, and engagement changes which pages receive high priority.

03

Weak picks exist

Very low-impression pages can rank highly because poor position contributes to the score.

04

Future outcome unavailable

The June-only release cannot establish future improvement or decline.

OBSERVED

The two ranking strategies produce different queues.

DIRECTIONAL

Multiple signals change page prioritization under the defined weights.

DECISION-SUPPORT

The ranking can help editors decide where to begin review.

NOT CLAIMED

Future performance, causal impact, or Google's ranking algorithm.
