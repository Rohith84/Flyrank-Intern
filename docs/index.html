Scoring for Search Optimization

A transparent, multi-signal ranking framework for helping content teams decide which pages deserve review and optimization first.

FlyRank ML Internship · June 2026 warehouse release · Decision-support analysis
View results View repository
ABSTRACT

Question → Method → Result

Question. Which content pages should be prioritized for review when a content team has more pages than it can refresh at once?

Method. I developed a transparent Opportunity Score combining impressions, click-through rate, average search position, and engagement per session, and compared its ranking with a simpler Week-4 baseline using the same client-grouped evaluation population.

Data. The analysis used the June 2026 FlyRank internship warehouse release, containing 11,694,072 raw records that were aggregated into 409,205 page-level observations across 65 clients.

Result. The Opportunity Score produced a substantially different ranking from the baseline: none of the baseline's top 50 pages appeared in the Opportunity Score's top 50.

Interpretation. This is evidence of a different prioritization strategy, not evidence that the Opportunity Score predicts future performance better, because the available release contains only June 2026 and therefore has no future outcome window.

Content teams cannot manually review every page at the same time. The practical decision is therefore not simply whether a page performs well or poorly, but which pages should enter the refresh queue first.

03 · METHODOLOGY

A transparent score, not a black box

The project uses weighted scoring and ranking rather than supervised prediction. The goal is to create a readable prioritization mechanism that an editor can inspect.

OPPORTUNITY SCORE
Score = 0.35 × Visibility + 0.30 × CTR Opportunity + 0.20 × Position Opportunity + 0.15 × Engagement Opportunity

Each component is normalized using the training-group range before the weighted score is calculated.

Impressions 35%
CTR 30%
Position 20%
Engagement 15%

Validation design

52 Train clients
13 Test clients
0 Client overlap
80/20 Grouped split

Leakage controls

Client and content identifiers were not used as features. No future outcome was available or introduced. Fields such as trend_direction and trend_pct were not used because they are absent from this warehouse release.

Unit of analysis

The raw daily performance records were aggregated into one observation per client-content pair for the June observation window.

06 · INTERPRETATION & LIMITATIONS

What the score gets wrong

A useful ranking system should expose its weak recommendations, not hide them.

01
WEAK RECOMMENDATION PATTERN

Very low-visibility pages can rank highly

Several top-ranked examples have only 1–3 impressions while having very poor average search positions. Because poor position contributes positively to the Opportunity Score, these pages can enter the queue even when their actual visibility is extremely low.

This is a clear case where the score should not be used automatically. An editor should review the page's actual context before allocating optimization effort.

01

No future outcome

The June-only release cannot establish whether a page later declines or improves.

02

No causal claim

The analysis cannot show that refreshing a page causes better search performance.

03

Manual weights

The Opportunity Score uses manually assigned feature weights rather than learned coefficients.

04

Human review required

The score is a prioritization aid, not an automatic content-editing decision.

HONEST FRAMING

Observed: the two ranking strategies produce different queues.

Directional: the multi-signal score changes which pages appear to have opportunity under the defined weighting scheme.

Decision-support: the ranking can help editors decide where to begin review.

Not claimed: future ranking prediction, causal impact, or prediction of Google's ranking algorithm.

07 · RANKED RECOMMENDATIONS

An action playbook for editors

The score should be treated as a queue for investigation, not an automatic command to change content.

01
HIGH PRIORITY

Review visible pages with weak CTR

Pages with meaningful impressions but weak CTR are practical candidates for reviewing titles, descriptions, search intent alignment, and presentation.

REVIEW
02
HIGH PRIORITY

Investigate pages with poor search position

Pages receiving impressions while ranking far from the strongest positions can be investigated for content relevance, internal linking, and coverage.

AUDIT
03
MEDIUM PRIORITY

Use engagement as supporting evidence

Engagement should help contextualize the search signals rather than being treated as an independent reason to refresh a page.

CONTEXTUALIZE
04
CAUTION

Do not automatically prioritize zero-visibility pages

Extremely low-impression pages can receive high scores because of their poor search position. These recommendations require additional editorial validation.
